# Part B speedup attribution

## Timing design

The Python timing blocks use the pinned tutorial matrices, 32 tones, 16 transmit and receive antennas, 16 streams, four users, an 8 dB SINR target and a 100 W cap. All 13 configurations used the same new channel draws, seeds 16001–16032. Seed 15999 warmed each block and was excluded from the intervals. A seeded order controls arm position within each draw. The timing blocks ran sequentially on the same Xeon Platinum 8573C cloud VM with one or two pinned logical CPUs. HiGHS used one native solver thread for each restricted master. In B5 the serial and parallel conditions use one and two tone workers, each with one BLAS thread. Hardware, versions and settings are logged in the environment manifests.

The joint SDP uses spectral separation over the tutorial's real sensing and complex user PSD blocks. HiGHS solves the restricted LP and the full-matrix pricing check supplies the upper bound. The native SCS 3.2.11 warm-up cross-check solved the first B1 fixed-receiver SDP from the certified point. This is a separate diagnostic. The timed B1–B5 rows use the certified eigencut implementation. The B4 comparison uses the persistent simplex master in a separate timing block so that both B3 and B4 reuse the simplex basis. The initial rebuilt-master B4 runs remain in the raw timing blocks. Each B4 outer update solves one Charnes–Cooper SDP, with its internal restricted master solves counted separately.

B5 changes the execution of each Dinkelbach update. At a trial global power price, independently optimized tone covariances give a dual upper bound. When power jumps across the budget, convex interpolation between price-bracket endpoints gives a feasible primal covariance. The report counts Dinkelbach SDPs, price evaluations, bisection steps and local restricted masters. The parallel worker clocks overlap. The exclusive wall-time breakdown uses the parallel tone stage, and the JSON retains summed worker-stage times separately.

The fixed, random and analytic LP arms use one shared feasibility gate, the same recovery and receiver updates, and exactly the same LP solve caps. Random and analytic pricing add the same number of columns at each tone, stream owner and nonterminal LP. The B7 timing includes generation of the directions and excludes the preparation of its matched per-draw schedule. Preparation time is in `schedules.jsonl`. The terminal B8 pricing pass evaluates the bound for the last fixed-receiver solve, with zero added columns. The baseline recovery raised a divide-by-zero warning on an intermediate candidate with zero minimum desired SINR. The physical acceptance gate handles that candidate and the reported outcomes use the returned streams.

The causal contrasts are B1/B2 for initialization, B2/B3 for caps, B3/B4 for the fixed-receiver transmit formulation, B3/B5 for decomposition, B6/B7 for adding random columns and B7/B8 for selecting the directions. B1/B8 is descriptive because the two families also differ in the sensing cone, the small safety margins and when streams are recovered and accepted. Part A's discrepancy with the tutorial plot remains unresolved. Raw matched times describe this Python stack.

## Findings

The selected Python timing cohort has 832 attempted rows and 824 physically feasible, certified returns. The inequalities and whole-matrix primal–dual gap gate were checked for every accepted SDP update. The B7/B8 column counts agree on every matched draw. A separate warm-up comparison reproduced the original Python solver's forced-budget B8 score.

- The structured warm start gave single- and two-thread median paired speedups of 1.44× and 1.40×. Single-thread warm-start speedup had a 95% CI of 1.35–1.55×. The median paired true-SCNR change was −0.021 dB. The 3×2 cap then gave another 1.50× in the single-thread comparison against the structured uncapped arm, with a median paired true-SCNR change of −0.101 dB. The 5×3 cap gave 1.02×, with its CI overlapping one.
- With a persistent master on both sides, Charnes–Cooper gave single-thread paired ratios of 1.12× at three outer iterations and 1.13× at five. The median paired true-SCNR changes were within 0.001 dB. The rebuilt B4 implementation was slower, illustrating the effect of master reuse on this comparison.
- The per-tone implementation had median wall times of 3.361 s serial and 2.949 s with two tone workers. The median paired serial-to-parallel ratio was 1.15× [1.12, 1.18]. Its successful trajectories used a median of six Dinkelbach solves, 31 price evaluations and 19 bisections. The corresponding joint 3×2 SDP took 0.531 s and 0.520 s in the two settings. The per-tone method produced nearly the same scores on valid pairs. It certified 28 of 32 draws in each timing block. Two local masters returned HiGHS status 15 and two returned dual values below the restricted primal by more than the gate. All four seeds certified under the separate cold-master retry in each setting. The timed ratios retain the original matched coverage.
- Random additions changed median paired true SCNR by less than 0.001 dB at both budgets. At matched counts, analytic placement increased median true SCNR by 1.747 dB at 3×2 and 1.884 dB at 5×3. Single-thread 95% CIs were 1.673–1.880 dB and 1.808–2.060 dB. The pricing stage added wall time relative to randomly generating directions.
- The end-to-end single-thread ratio from B1 to B8 was 6.87× [6.43, 7.29] at 3×2 and 2.48× [2.40, 2.59] at 5×3. The corresponding paired median true-SCNR differences were −0.219 and −0.052 dB. All 32 draw pairs were within both registered Part A quality limits at each budget. These are descriptive results for the cloud Python stack.
