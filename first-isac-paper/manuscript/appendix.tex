\section{Inputs, Provenance and Historical Timing}
The input is the tutorial of Liu \emph{et al.}~\cite{liu2026clutter}, code revision \texttt{0f864c4} and distributed \texttt{covMat\_BLP.mat}, with the full pin and SHA-256 in the run manifests. The static benchmark fixes the target at \(-10^\circ\) and 41.8 m, with \(G_t=G_r=100\) and \(\sigma_{\rm rcs}=10^{0.5}\). It uses \(\sigma_c^2=10^{-19}\Delta f\), uniform integer user distances of 50--400 m, and circular Gaussian channels of variance \(10^{[-30-27\log_{10}(d_k)]/10}\) at distance \(d_k\) in metres. Independent channel and initialization seeds permit matched replays.

For calibration, the least-squares kernel maps vectorized probing transmit covariances to estimated receive covariances. In column-major indexing, the Choi rearrangement maps entry \((a+(b-1)N_r,i+(j-1)N_t)\) of \(V\) to entry \((a+(i-1)N_r,b+(j-1)N_r)\) of \(\mathcal J(V)\), with receive indices \(a,b\) and transmit indices \(i,j\). Clipping the Hermitian Choi spectrum and applying the calibration floor precede the inverse rearrangement. The ideal independent-scatterer map already has a PSD Choi matrix, as a sum of outer products of vectorized rank-one Kraus factors.

\section{Ablation Implementations and Timing Scope}
The joint Python SDP enforces real symmetric sensing and complex Hermitian user cones by spectral separation. Dinkelbach iteration~\cite{dinkelbach} updates the fractional objective. A restricted LP supplies a feasible PSD representation and full-matrix separation bounds the SDP value. HiGHS solves the restricted LP. A native SCS 3.2.11 warm-up is a separate subproblem cross-check. The MATLAB driver and static parser checks are included. Measured MATLAB/CVX/MOSEK times, backend tolerance equivalence and parsing overhead require a licensed run.

B4 uses one Charnes--Cooper matrix SDP per outer update~\cite{charnes}. Its persistent-master single-thread ratios are \(1.12\times\) [1.09, 1.17] and \(1.13\times\) [1.06, 1.17] at three and five steps. Rebuilding the B4 master each solve slowed the variant. The \(5\times3\) cap alone gives \(1.02\times\) [0.99, 1.08]. B5 decomposes the solve per tone with a bisected global price. When power jumps across the budget, convex interpolation between the bracket endpoints gives a feasible covariance. Two workers give \(1.15\times\) [1.12, 1.18] versus the serial variant. Both B5 timing blocks retained 28/32 draws. Two rejected masters reported HiGHS status 15 and two failed the primal--dual gap gate. An untimed cold-master diagnostic certified all four seeds. The original timed coverage is retained.

B6 holds the initial bank fixed. B7 and B8 append matching counts by tone, owner and nonterminal iteration. B7 includes random direction generation and excludes precomputing the matched schedule. Other wall time is retained in the logs. The complete ladder, two-thread figures, feasible counts and paired bootstrap intervals are in the Part B summary.

\section{Numerical Certificate and Atom Activity}
The reference continuation holds the last receiver, objective scale and constraints fixed, adding directions with excess above \(10^{-11}\). It stops at audited upper-to-feasible gap \(10^{-4}\) dB. All 288 references converge in 4--21 solves. The upper calculation uses the actual normalization dual \(\psi_D\), nonnegative multipliers, full-sphere excess and the scale-variable violation. A bound derived from the smallest scaled clutter eigenvalue limits the scale. Independent feasible witnesses mix with strict ZF powers when required. The audit uses floating-point padding.

Two \(5\times3\) simplex allocations showed large decoded QoS shortfalls relative to normalized noise despite reported zero row residuals. They were seed 10005 at \(K=6\), 8 dB and seed 10020 at \(K=4\), 12 dB. Independent HiGHS interior-point re-solves bracketed all 288 restricted objectives within \(7.02\times10^{-10}\) dB. The largest discrepancy from the archived raw objective was \(2.43\times10^{-9}\) dB. All recovered streams passed the physical gate. The atom activity analysis uses the original decoded powers.

At \(3\times2\), the per-condition median addition count was 649--968 and 84.0--85.8\% of priced atoms ever received power above \(10^{-8}P_s\). At \(5\times3\), those ranges were 1,680--2,506 and 89.0--89.8\%. A basic optimum has few positive variables, which limits the terminal active fraction. Initial atoms carry all decoded power at \(1\times1\), and priced atoms carry all decoded terminal power at the two larger budgets. The paired true gains over the fixed bank in the end-to-end control are 1.747 dB [1.682, 1.880] and 1.884 dB [1.813, 2.060] at \(3\times2\) and \(5\times3\). Count-matched random controls give the same medians to three decimals. The separate fixed-receiver controls and all 408,649 terminal atom records are in the Part D archive.

\section{Learning-to-Optimize Protocol and Results}
Synthetic maps independently draw scatterer bearings in \([-90^\circ,90^\circ]\), resample the archived per-tone power profiles and apply independent mean-one lognormal factors of log standard deviation 0.35. The ideal PSD Choi factorization differs from the explicit pilot projection by \(1.93\times10^{-15}\) relative Frobenius norm. Synthetic scenes use 3--6 users and a uniform integer threshold of 6--14 dB. The original map uses the archived estimated/ideal pair at four users and 8 dB. Training IDs are 510000--514999, held-out map IDs 520000--520063, and original-map draw IDs 530000--530063. The protocol records separate PCG64 streams for maps, channels and optimization.

The teacher alternates exact MVDR and tightened covariance column generation. Its terminal fixed-receiver gap and upper bound on remaining alternating gain both meet 0.01 dB. The stacked teacher and deployment filters have a median angle of 69.88 degrees over training scenes. The deployed features are the first physics master's dual quantities at the ZF-start MVDR filters. Local Rayleigh features and channel overlaps are combined with log noise and QoS and mean-pooled user features. A shared \(12\to8\to4\) tanh network produces real coefficients over four unit complex directions, with a fixed target coefficient bias of 1.25.

The first loss minimizes the negative first-master reduced cost of the predicted unit vector with a fixed per-sample normalization. The second loss adds a normalized teacher Lagrangian quadratic on the same predicted direction, weighted by each user owner's total active scaled LP power. The envelope derivative of the objective with respect to the atom is its scaled power times the derivative of its Lagrangian coefficient at fixed dual variables. Teacher matrices and masses enter only the loss. Both policies use five epochs, minibatches of 32 scenes and Adam at learning rate 0.003 with \((\beta_1,\beta_2)=(0.9,0.999)\) and numerical stabilizer \(10^{-8}\)~\cite{adam}. The maximum scaled centered finite-difference error on three pilot scenes was \(4.66\times10^{-11}\).

\section{Reproduction Map}
