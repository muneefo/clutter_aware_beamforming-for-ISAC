1    Architecture and information assumptions
A monostatic base station (BS) uses fixed transmit and receive arrays to serve K single-antenna downlink
users and sense one point target. Environmental reflections enter the sensing receiver through the
same transmitted signal. The design question is how accurately a finite probing interval predicts the
interference seen by a new transmit beam. The design variables are the downlink precoder and one
spatial receive filter.

Information at the BS. During one interval the BS knows its element coordinates, the user channels,
the nominal target response and the receiver noise power. An environmental map supplies L candidate
clutter positions. The baseline treats these positions as exact and estimates their nonnegative reflec-
tion powers from echoes. Power statistics and geometry persist from calibration into operation. The
target location is supplied by an external estimate or preceding acquisition stage. The sensing task is
maintaining target echo quality at that location.
Receiver assumptions. Narrowband, synchronized, spatial processing uses a common delay reference
for the modeled returns. Transmit leakage has been cancelled to a negligible level, or a separately
calibrated residual can be included in the known noise covariance. The baseline uses spatially white
noise. Clutter-aware covariance modeling follows the physical principle in [1]. The fixed-array geometry
and observation protocol below specify this paper’s proposed model.

Measurements. Vary probe diversity B and snapshots Sb separately. Assess covariance prediction
on held-out beams, then measure true SCNR, sensing-bound coverage, per-user violations and feasible
outcomes on new geometries. Report solver time alongside probing, estimation and checking costs. The
latter determine the observation-to-action budget. The unobserved true powers are available to the
evaluator and ideal reference only.
